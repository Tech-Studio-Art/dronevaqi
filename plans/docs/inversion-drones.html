Inversion mixer drones

On calm winter nights Delhi's smoke sits in a cold layer 50–150 m deep, under a lid of warmer, cleaner air. These two airframes are built to break that lid. The heavy-lift drone pushes clean air from aloft down to street level. The quad flies first to measure, and the fleet mixes only when the air above really is cleaner. There are no filters: the downwash itself is the product.

Breathing-zone PM after 30 min, 100 heavy drones: −16 % (at 60 m) to −23 % (at 90 m) Same fleet carrying filters: −1.5 % PM2.5 Zone: 500 × 500 m, 50 m grid

How a mixing night works

SHEET 0 · MISSION

① Profile

A scout quad climbs at 1 m/s to 300 m, logging PM2.5, PM10, temperature and humidity every 10 m. The temperature profile shows where the lid sits; the PM profile shows whether the air above is cleaner.

② Decide

The fleet mixes only when PM aloft is clearly lower than at street level. When a stubble-smoke layer rides above the lid, mixing would bring it down, so the fleet stays on the ground.

③ Mix

Heavy drones hover inside the inversion on a 50 m grid. Each one pulls clean air from above its rotors and drives it to the ground as a jet; the ring of return flow stirs the whole cell.

Battery is the payload

Mixing needs thrust, not cargo. The 25 kg payload allowance becomes a second battery pack (about 4 kWh usable at 200 Wh/kg). Take-off mass and thrust stay the same, so hover power stays 6.8 kW while flight time rises to roughly 55 min.

Big, slow rotors

The mixing effect depends on jet momentum, which equals thrust. Power for a given thrust falls as disc area grows, so the largest practical rotors give the same mixing for the least energy. Coaxial pairs keep the footprint inside a 3 m box.

Sense the air it moves

The mast inlet sits 0.5 m above the upper rotors, in the undisturbed air the drone is about to push down. Comparing that reading with street-level monitors tells the fleet, every minute, whether mixing is still helping.

The quad's jet has 1/25 of the heavy drone's momentum and reaches only about 24 m into the same inversion. In the CFD, 100 quads cut breathing-zone PM by 2 % in 30 minutes against 16 % for 100 heavy drones. Its value is the measurement that decides whether the heavy fleet flies at all.

Simulated performance

CFD · 100 DRONES · 500 × 500 m

At 60 m the heavy fleet acts within 5–10 minutes. At 90 m the drones draw the cleanest air, but their jets need about 20 minutes to work down to street level. Either way the ceiling is set by how much cleaner the air aloft is: full mixing of the lowest 100 m in this scenario gives 25 %.

With a light wind the zone's air is replaced every 500 m ÷ wind speed. The lasting benefit is then about three quarters of the 30-minute figure at 0.3 m/s and about half at 1 m/s.

Model: axisymmetric unit-cell URANS with Boussinesq buoyancy and two k-ε turbulence models in OpenFOAM, one drone per 50 m cell. OPEN QUESTIONS
  • How often on November nights is the air at 60–150 m actually cleaner than at street level? The scout profiles answer this before any heavy drone is built.
  • How much of the simulated 20 % survives real stable-air turbulence and light wind? Measure with ground monitors inside and outside the zone on the same night.
  • Noise from 100 heavy drones over homes at night has not been modelled. Measure one airframe at 60 m before planning a fleet.
  • Coarse dust (PM10) settles about 9 m per 30 minutes; the simulation ignored this, which slightly understates the benefit for PM10.
  • Flying at night, and profiling above 120 m, needs permission under India's Drone Rules 2021, and much of Delhi is a yellow or red zone. Pick a green-zone pilot site on the city edge.
